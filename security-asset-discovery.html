Asset Discovery · Nmap + Shodan

ICS Asset Discovery & Exposure

A hands-on lab in the first rule of OT security: you can't protect what you can't see. I build an accurate inventory of industrial assets, fingerprint their services, and quantify how much of that attack surface is exposed — with Nmap for discovery and Shodan for internet-facing ICS.

01 Objective

To produce a trustworthy OT asset inventory — device, vendor, firmware, open services and protocol — and to measure exposure across the Purdue model. Coming from product development, I know these devices are fragile; the goal is complete visibility without disrupting a running process.

02 What I Do

  • Passive first. Start from traffic captures and ARP/DHCP data to discover devices without sending a single probe to sensitive equipment.

  • Careful, OT-safe active scanning. Where authorized, use Nmap with conservative timing and targeted probes to fingerprint hosts, services and industrial ports — never aggressive scans that can crash a PLC.

  • Find internet exposure. Use Shodan to identify industrial devices, HMIs and protocols reachable from the internet — the exposures attackers find first.

  • Classify by Purdue level. Place each asset in its zone — field devices, controllers, supervisory, operations — and map the conduits between them.

  • Quantify the attack surface. Turn the inventory into a prioritized exposure report — what's reachable, from where, and what should never be.

03 Tools & Techniques

Discovery
Nmap (host discovery, service/version detection, conservative timing templates), passive discovery from packet captures
Exposure
Shodan queries & filters for ICS ports/protocols (Modbus 502, BACnet 47808, etc.), banners and geolocation
Fingerprinting
Vendor / device / firmware identification, open-service enumeration, default-service recognition
Output
Asset inventory, Purdue-level classification, zone & conduit map, prioritized exposure report

04 IEC 62443 Alignment

  • Asset inventory. A complete, accurate inventory is the foundation the whole standard builds on — you can't define zones or assess risk without it.

  • Zones & conduits (ISA/IEC 62443-3-2). Discovery feeds directly into partitioning the system into security zones and defining the conduits between them.

  • Exposure reduction. Internet-facing ICS findings drive the highest-priority remediation — removing or firewalling exposed control interfaces.
