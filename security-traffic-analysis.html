OT Network Traffic Analysis

A hands-on lab where I capture and dissect real industrial protocol traffic — Modbus/TCP, BACnet and MQTT — to baseline normal operation and detect anomalies, unauthorized writes and reconnaissance. Built to develop and demonstrate OT monitoring capability in a safe, authorized environment.

01 Objective

To build practical skill in monitoring operational-technology networks — understanding what "normal" looks like on industrial protocols so that malicious or anomalous activity stands out. I focus on the exact protocols I built products on (Modbus, BACnet, MQTT), which lets me read the traffic at the field level, not just the packet level.

02 What I Do

  • Capture safely. Collect traffic passively via a SPAN port / network TAP so monitoring never interferes with a live control process.

  • Dissect the protocol. Use Wireshark's Modbus, BACnet and MQTT dissectors and custom display filters to read function codes, register reads/writes, object properties and topics.

  • Baseline normal. Profile who talks to whom, on which registers, how often — building a picture of legitimate polling and control patterns.

  • Flag anomalies. Spot unauthorized write function codes, unexpected masters, malformed PDUs, replayed commands and scanning bursts — the fingerprints of an attack on an ICS.

  • Script it. Automate extraction with tshark to pull indicators from large captures for repeatable analysis.

03 Tools & Techniques

Capture
Wireshark, tshark, dumpcap; passive collection via SPAN port / network TAP
Protocols
Modbus/TCP (function codes, coils/registers), BACnet/IP (objects & properties), MQTT (topics, retained messages)
Analysis
Display filters, Follow TCP stream, IO graphs, Expert Info, protocol hierarchy, conversation statistics
Detection focus
Unauthorized writes, rogue masters, replay, malformed PDUs, port/register scanning, cleartext credentials

04 IEC 62443 Alignment

Passive monitoring and traffic baselining map directly to the standard's requirements for visibility and detection:

  • FR 6 — Timely response to events. Continuous monitoring and audit of control-system communications.

  • FR 7 — Resource availability. Detecting floods and scanning that threaten the availability of a control process.

  • Zones & conduits. Traffic profiling validates that communication stays within its intended conduit and no unexpected flows cross zone boundaries.
