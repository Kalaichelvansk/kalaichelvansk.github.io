OT Vulnerability Assessment

A hands-on lab turning an asset inventory into a prioritized, actionable risk picture — identifying default credentials, unpatched firmware and insecure services with Nessus, then ranking findings by their impact on the physical process and mapping remediation to IEC 62443.

01 Objective

To assess OT assets for weaknesses and translate raw findings into a risk-ranked remediation plan the business can act on. In OT, severity isn't just CVSS — a "medium" on a device that controls a physical process can outrank a "critical" on an isolated workstation. Having built these controllers, I can judge that real-world impact.

02 What I Do

  • Scan safely. Use Nessus with OT-aware policies — throttled, scheduled during maintenance windows, and preferring credentialed or passive checks over aggressive probes that could disrupt production.

  • Find the classics. Default and weak credentials, unauthenticated protocols, end-of-life firmware, exposed management services and missing patches.

  • Correlate. Match findings to CVEs and vendor / ICS-CERT advisories to confirm exploitability and available fixes.

  • Prioritize by impact. Score risk on both CVSS and consequence to the process — safety, availability and integrity — not severity alone.

  • Recommend realistic fixes. Where a device can't be patched, propose compensating controls — segmentation, access control, monitoring — that reduce risk without touching a running process.

03 Tools & Techniques

Scanning
Nessus — credentialed & uncredentialed checks, OT-safe scan policies, throttled timing, scheduled windows
Intelligence
CVE database, vendor security advisories, ICS-CERT / CISA ICS advisories
Risk scoring
CVSS combined with process-impact rating (safety / availability / integrity), likelihood vs. consequence
Deliverable
Prioritized findings report, remediation & compensating-control plan, IEC 62443 mapping

04 IEC 62443 Alignment

  • Risk assessment (62443-3-2). Findings feed the zone/conduit risk assessment that sets each zone's target security level (SL-T).

  • Security levels. Compare a zone's capability (SL-C) against its target to expose gaps and justify controls.

  • Patch & compensating controls (62443-2-3). Realistic OT patch management, with mitigations where patching isn't possible.
